Sense Guidelines
- essere: express finite forms as clauses like “I am”, “he/she/it is”, “we were”, “you (plural) would be”; participles/gerunds become “been”, “being”, etc.
- avere: clauses such as “I have”, “he/she/it has”, “they will have”, “we would have”; non-finite forms “to have”, “having”, “had”.
- stare: clauses like “I am staying”, “they were staying”, or more neutral “they stay” / “they are”; non-finite “to stay”, “staying”, “stayed”.
 - potere: clauses such as “I can; I am able to”, “they could; they were able to”, “you (plural) will be able to”. Make the copula agree with the subject:
   - Present “able to” → am (I), is (he/she/it), are (you, we, they)
   - Past “able to” → was (I, he/she/it), were (you, we, they)
 - dovere: express obligation/advisability with person-aware clauses, and reflect tense/mood nuances succinctly:
   - Imperfect (ongoing/past expectation, Tense=Imp): “I had to; I was supposed to”, “you had to; you were supposed to”, etc. (match the subject)
   - Subjunctive or hypothetical (Mood=Sub, often in ‘se’ clauses): “if I should have to”, “if you should have to” (match subject)
   - Remote/perfective past (passato remoto, Tense=Past on simple forms like dovetti/dovette/dovettero): add completion nuance “had to (and did)” with the subject
   - Conditional (Mood=Cnd): “I would have to; I should”, “we would have to; we should”, etc.
   - Non‑finite: “to have to”, “having to”, “had to” (participles/gerunds), and apply clitic rules with [VERB] when present
- volere: clauses like “I want; I want to”, “they would want; they would like to”, “you (plural) will want”.
- Modal or periphrastic forms should retain the auxiliary focus rather than lexical translations (e.g., “be going to” is acceptable if idiomatically necessary, but avoid full clause paraphrases).
