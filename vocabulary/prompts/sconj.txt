Style and Formatting (must follow exactly)
- Lowercase the gloss unless sentence context requires capitalization (rare).
- Separate multiple distinct senses with semicolons, ordered from most general to most contextual. Maximum three senses.
- Use natural English connectors (“because”, “since”, “so that”, “even though”, “when”) rather than literal paraphrases.
- If the conjunction needs a paired structure ("sia che", "più ... che", "come se"), write the full English pattern (“whether ... or”, “more ... than”, “as if”) even if only part appears in the text column, so learners know the complete frame.
- Avoid full sentences, explanations, or quotation marks. Parentheses should be reserved for short register or usage notes like “(formal)” when sentences clearly demand it.

Sense Guidelines
- Causal: perché, poiché, siccome → because; since. quando used causally should reflect that (“since”).
- Temporal: quando, mentre, finché, appena, prima che, dopo che → translate to “when”, “while”, “until”, “as soon as”, “before”, “after”.
- Conditional: se, qualora, purché → “if”, “provided that”, “as long as”.
- Purpose: affinché, perché (when meaning "so that"), onde → “so that”, “in order that”.
- Concessive: anche se, sebbene, benché, pure → “even though”, “although”, “though”.
- Comparative and manner: come, come se, quasi che → “as”, “as if”, “as though”.
- Reported speech/complementizers: che (when introducing clauses), di (when SCONJ) → “that”, “to” (for infinitival clauses).
- Correlative/composite structures (e.g., "sia ... che", "tanto ... quanto", "più ... che") should be glossed with both halves to show the full pattern.

Decision Rules
- Base each gloss on the example sentences, defaulting to the most common dictionary use when sentences are neutral.
- Limit to at most three senses; add a second or third only when distinct readings are clearly evidenced.
- Keep wording parallel for related forms (perché/perciò) so learners can compare easily.
- Spell-check carefully and use American English (“behavior” not “behaviour”).
